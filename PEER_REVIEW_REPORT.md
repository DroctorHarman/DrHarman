## 1. OVERALL QUALITY AND ORIGINALITY

### 1.1 Summary of the manuscript as submitted

The manuscript reports a single-center, single-surgeon, two-arm, 1:1 randomized trial comparing a "large titanium ligation clip described as LT400-equivalent" with a "non-absorbable polymeric locking clip described as Hem-o-Lok" for appendiceal stump closure in laparoscopic appendectomy. The abstract reports 200 records (100 per recorded group), mean operative time of 43.28 min (SD 8.05) for titanium versus 56.72 min (SD 9.24) for the group labeled "Hemoclip" (difference −13.44 min; nominal 95% CI −15.86 to −11.02), mean hospital stay 1.85 versus 2.22 days, complications in 6 versus 7 cases, no open conversions, and one 30-day readmission in the comparator group only.

The manuscript's own framing substantially undercuts its claims. The abstract states: "This draft reports a provisional reanalysis of the supplied 200-row extract. The extract has not been reconciled with earlier reports, and trial conduct and device identity remain to be verified against source records." The conclusions state: "conflicting source summaries and an unusual cross-arm pattern require reconciliation before this can be interpreted as a randomized treatment effect. The sparse complication data do not establish safety equivalence."

### 1.2 Strengths

- The clinical question (which mechanical closure strategy is faster and safer) is plausible and clinically relevant to operating-room workflow.
- The comparative device question (titanium ligation clip vs polymeric locking clip) is under-represented in the literature relative to clip-versus-ligature comparisons.
- A priori structure is present: eligibility criteria, exclusions, block randomization description, primary endpoint definition, and secondary outcomes are all articulated.
- The tone is appropriately cautious where the data warrant it (e.g., explicit caution about the Figure 2 cross-study comparison; explicit statement that safety equivalence is not established).
- References are largely recent (including WSES Jerusalem Guidelines 2025 Edition and CONSORT 2025), indicating awareness of current standards.

### 1.4 Originality assessment

The question is not novel. Prior work directly addresses appendiceal stump closure with clips: a four-arm randomized trial of a titanium DS clip vs Hem-o-Lok vs endoloop vs stapler (Delibegović & Mehmedović, 2018, cited as ref [9]); a retrospective head-to-head of 179 patients comparing titanium clips with Hem-o-Lok (Uçar & Bulut, 2024, ref [10]); randomized and nonrandomized polymeric-clip studies (refs [17, 19–23]); and meta-analyses of clips vs ligatures/endoloops (refs [16, 18, 26]). The manuscript correctly notes that the DS clip in ref [9] "should not be assumed interchangeable" with the device studied here, and that prior clip–endoloop meta-analyses "cannot establish a titanium-versus-Hem-o-Lok effect" (Discussion, pp.6–7).

However, the manuscript's central estimate (−13.44 min) is in sharp conflict with the only directly comparable published head-to-head: Uçar & Bulut (2024) reported mean operative times of 53.1 min (titanium) vs 52.4 min (Hem-o-Lok) — a much smaller contrast in the opposite direction. The manuscript never reconciles this discrepancy, nor does it explain why a single-center reanalysis of an unverified extract would outweigh prior direct evidence. Incremental originality is therefore low unless the data are independently verified.

### 1.5 Scholarly significance

Conditional on verification, a confirmed ~13-minute reduction in total operative time would be operationally relevant. Significance is currently not established because the contrast cannot be attributed to the device (total operative time is not closure time), randomization conduct is unverified, and the data pattern is anomalous.

**Assessment:** Originality — low to moderate; scholarly significance — conditional and unverified; overall quality as submitted — insufficient for publication.

## 2. CRITICAL REVIEW OF THE LITERATURE REVIEW

### 2.1 Relevance and currency

The cited literature is thematically relevant: laparoscopic appendectomy as standard approach [2]; stump-closure methods [4]; early clip work [5]; Cochrane review [6]; device-variation syntheses [7, 8]; direct device comparisons [9, 10, 15, 17, 20]; and harms reporting standards [11, 28–30]. Citation years run to 2026 (e.g., WSES Jerusalem Guidelines 2025 Edition, ref [2]; CONSORT 2025, ref [30]), consistent with the review date of 21 November 2026.

**Concern requiring verification:** Several references carry 2025–2026 publication dates (refs [2], [8?], [14], [30]). These should be verified against the original sources before submission; the reviewer cannot confirm their existence or exact bibliographic detail from the supplied PDF alone.

### 2.2 Depth of critical analysis and synthesis

The literature review is enumerative rather than synthetic. Studies are listed serially with minimal comparative appraisal (e.g., refs [14–27] are catalogued in two paragraphs with little differentiation of design, risk of bias, or effect direction). Critically:

- The review fails to state *why this trial is needed* given existing direct comparisons. Ref [10] (Uçar & Bulut, n = 179) already compared titanium clips with Hem-o-Lok and found near-identical operative times. Ref [20] (Lv et al.) found no significant difference in operative duration between absorbable and non-absorbable clips. The manuscript does not weigh its own (−13.44 min) estimate against this body of evidence.
- Heterogeneity across prior studies (different devices, definitions of operative time, inclusion of complicated vs uncomplicated appendicitis, single vs multicenter) is acknowledged only generically ("Evidence syntheses describe substantial variation in devices, patient selection and outcome definitions [7, 8]") without a table or structured comparison that would position this study.
- The theoretical framework is not articulated: no stated model linking clip mechanism (titanium ligation vs polymeric locking) to operative-time or stump-integrity outcomes, no rationale for the selected primary endpoint derived from prior work.

### 2.3 Clarity of the research gap

The gap is stated as: "The clinical question is whether the two specified closure strategies differ in total operative duration and what short-term harms are observed" (Introduction, p.2). This is under-specified: total operative duration is a surrogate heavily confounded by non-closure steps; the harms question is under-powered by design (13 events); and the review does not define the minimal clinically important difference in operative time.

### 2.4 Weak arguments and unsupported claims

- "A Cochrane review later judged the evidence insufficient to recommend a specific mechanical closure device over another [6]" — accurately reported, but the manuscript then proceeds as though its own unverified reanalysis resolves that insufficiency.
- "A retrospective head-to-head study of 179 patients also compared titanium clips with Hem-o-Lok [10]" — cited without reporting its near-null result (53.1 vs 52.4 min) in the review; the conflicting magnitude is disclosed only later in the Discussion (p.6).
- "The study was designed to estimate the difference in total operative time between the two locally used clip strategies" — design intent cannot be confirmed (no protocol available).

### 2.5 Missing perspectives

- Pediatric subgroup (eligibility from age 6): no discussion of developmental anatomy, device sizing, or powered subgroup analysis; conclusions cannot extend to children.
- Complicated appendicitis: explicitly excluded from inference ("The export cannot support conclusions about complicated appendicitis," Discussion, p.8) yet not reflected in a clearly framed scope.
- Cost and resource use: "Mechanistic explanations and cost savings were not measured here" (Discussion, p.7) — yet the rationale for clip choice in a private hospital setting implicitly invokes efficiency/cost without measurement.
- Long-term device-related events (stump infection, hernia, clip migration): not addressed beyond one citation to Rasmussen et al. [29], which concerns appendectomy generally, not comparative late clip safety.

### 2.6 Recommendations

1. Rewrite the literature review as a structured synthesis: table prior device comparisons (design, device, n, operative-time results, complication results, risk of bias), then state precisely how this study adds verified, non-redundant evidence.
2. Explicitly reconcile the Uçar & Bulut (2024) near-null finding with the reported −13.44 min contrast before claiming incremental value.
3. Define and justify the primary endpoint (total operative time vs closure time) with reference to prior work; justify the minimal clinically important difference.
4. State the theoretical/clinical rationale for expecting titanium clips to outperform polymeric locking clips on total operative time.
5. Do not invent references; verify all 2025–2026 citations against original sources. (See unverified-concern note in §8.)

## 3. ASSESSMENT OF THE RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

### 3.1 Research design

A prospective, two-arm, 1:1 parallel randomized trial at a single center by one surgeon is an appropriate design for comparing closure devices and controls operator variation. However:

- Single-center/single-surgeon design limits external validity; no justification given for why a multicenter design was not feasible.
- The described period (2017–2026, ten calendar years) creates a major threat to internal validity: operative technique, anesthesia, device availability, and case mix may drift over a decade; no stratification by calendar period is reported.

### 3.2 Sampling strategy and participant flow

Eligibility (age ≥ 6 years, uncomplicated acute appendicitis, clearly identifiable appendiceal base ≤ 10 mm) and exclusions (severe base inflammation/necrosis, interval surgery, suspected neoplasm, pregnancy, primary open surgery indications) are clinically sensible. **Critical gap:** the number screened, number excluded, and number verified as randomized in original enrollment records were not available (Results, p.4). Without these, a CONSORT-compliant participant flow cannot be constructed, and selection bias cannot be assessed.

Allocation: described as computer-generated blocks of 10 with opaque, sequentially numbered envelopes opened after intraoperative eligibility assessment — appropriate in principle. **Major concern:** implementation is unverified; the author confirmed that all 200 EnvelopeNo values were reassigned by treatment arm for the export (001–100 Titanium; 101–200 Hemoclip), and "no subset retains a documented original opening-order number" (Allocation-record review, p.5). Thus the original allocation sequence and concealment implementation cannot be reconstructed, and the randomization itself cannot be audited.

### 3.3 Blinding

Surgeon blinding is impossible (device visible at application). Masking of participants, outcome assessors, and analysts is unverified. Because several outcomes (wound infection, ileus, readmission) involve subjective or ascertainment-dependent assessment, lack of verified blinding is a detection-bias risk, particularly for the primary composite of short-term outcomes.

### 3.4 Interventions

- Titanium: "large titanium ligation clip described as LT400-equivalent"; Polymeric: "non-absorbable polymeric locking clip described as Hem-o-Lok." Both groups reportedly underwent a standardized three-port approach.
- **Unverified:** model, size, applier, number of clips actually used, and whether the titanium device is genuinely LT400-equivalent (Interventions, p.3; Abstract, p.1: "device identity remain to be verified against source records"). The row-level export distinguishes only "Titanium" and "Hemoclip" — brand identity of the comparator is unverified.

### 3.5 Outcomes

- Primary: skin incision to final skin closure (total operative time). This does **not** isolate stump-closure time (Discussion, p.6); any observed difference may reflect dissection, instrumentation, case complexity, or secular changes rather than the clip.
- Secondary: complications, hospital stay, conversion to open surgery, 30-day readmission.
- **Unverified/undefined:** complication ascertainment method and observation window; severity grading; whether care outside CMC was captured; whether readmission was counted from surgery or discharge; the follow-up table dates events relative to discharge (Outcomes, p.3; Results, p.4).
- Harms reporting: zeroes are explicitly flagged as "no positive flags in the export, not verified absence of events" (Table 2 footnote, p.5) — non-compliant with CONSORT Harms 2022 [11], which requires harms to be reported by assigned group including zero counts only where systematic assessment is documented.

### 3.6 Sample size and power

No sample-size calculation is reported anywhere in the manuscript. With 13 total complication events, the study is grossly underpowered for safety; a non-significant risk difference (RD −1.0 pp; 95% CI −8.5 to 6.4) cannot support any safety or equivalence claim. No noninferiority margin is prespecified.

### 3.7 Ethical considerations

- Ethics approval: Research Ethics Committee of CMC Private Hospital, 3 January 2016 (approval no. 202), requiring written informed consent from participants or legal guardians (Declarations, p.9).
- **Unverified:** consent and pediatric assent procedures as implemented; whether approval covered the full described period (2017–2026); whether protocol amendments occurred.
- Trial registration: none identified (Declarations, p.9). Registration of an RCT before enrollment is a standard requirement; retrospective registration does not fully remediate this but must at least be disclosed.

### 3.8 Data analysis methods

- Categorical data: counts and percentages; continuous: means and SDs, medians and IQRs.
- Contrasts: titanium minus Hemoclip; Welch intervals for mean differences; Newcombe score intervals for risk differences; unadjusted, nominal, assuming independent observations; no missing values in fields used (Analysis, pp.3–4).
- **Major concerns:** not established as ITT; no per-protocol or as-treated sensitivity analysis; no adjusted model (13 events preclude complication modeling; operative time is a post-allocation variable); no prespecified analysis plan verification; multiple comparisons unaddressed.

### 3.9 Methodological biases and threats to credibility

- Selection bias: unscreenable flow; concealment unverifiable.
- Performance bias: unblinded surgeon; possible learning-curve effects over ten years.
- Detection bias: blinding of assessors unverified; subjective complications.
- Attrition bias: follow-up completeness asserted by the export but not verified against source records; readmission window undefined.
- Reporting bias: conflicting earlier outputs (38.40/52.60 min) unexplained; selective reporting of the final export cannot be excluded.
- Data-integrity threat: r = 0.993 cross-arm correlation with a constant 10–18 min arm offset after suffix sorting (p.6) is anomalous for two independently randomized arms and requires source-record explanation.

**Recommendations:** (1) obtain and audit the allocation log and operative records; (2) register the trial (prospectively if any data collection remains; otherwise disclose retrospective registration) and submit protocol + SAP; (3) reconstruct CONSORT flow; (4) define primary estimand (ITT) with pre-specified estimands and sensitivity analyses; (5) specify a noninferiority/equivalence margin for safety or explicitly disclaim safety conclusions; (6) document device models/sizes/appliers from operative records and device documentation.

Complication subtypes recorded: superficial wound infection (2 vs 2), prolonged ileus (2 vs 2), urinary retention (1 vs 1), seroma (1 vs 1), atelectasis (0 vs 1). Severity grades and stump-specific ascertainment unverified (Results, p.5).

The reported Welch mean-difference interval for operative time (−13.44 ± approx. 2.4 min) is arithmetically consistent with the reported means and SDs under the independence assumption (SE ≈ 1.23 min). The Newcombe risk-difference interval for complications is consistent with 6 vs 7 events per 100. These are **nominal, unadjusted intervals**; they do not establish validity of the underlying estimates.

### 4.2 Issues identifiable from the reported results

1. **No ITT analysis:** the analysis uses recorded group labels and is "not presented as a verified intention-to-treat analysis" (Results, p.4). With zero recorded crossovers, ITT and per-protocol would coincide *if* the recorded labels were trustworthy — but that trustworthiness is precisely what cannot be verified.
2. **Inconsistency with earlier outputs:** earlier summary means of 38.40 and 52.60 min are not reproduced; the correction of one readmission "did not change the operative-time comparison"; provenance of conflicting outputs unresolved (Data reconciliation, p.6).
3. **Anomalous cross-arm correlation:** r = 0.993 after numeric-suffix alignment, every Hemoclip value 10–18 min higher. "Shared suffixes do not establish clinical pairing. Source records are needed to explain the pattern and determine whether the independence assumption is appropriate" (p.6). If the two arms are in fact paired records (or one arm derived from the other), all nominal CIs and p-values are invalid.
4. **Primary endpoint mismatch:** total operative time ≠ closure time; result cannot be attributed to clips.
5. **Sparse-event problem:** 13 events total; the risk-difference CI spans clinically important harm and benefit; "a non-significant difference in sparse event counts is not a substitute" for a prespecified noninferiority/equivalence design (Discussion, p.8).
6. **Zero conversions, one readmission:** do not rule out uncommon harm or incomplete follow-up.
7. **Follow-up ambiguity:** dates relative to discharge; readmission counting origin unknown; out-of-hospital events potentially uncaptured.

### 4.3 Issues requiring access to original data or further verification

- Whether the r = 0.993 pattern reflects data fabrication, a pairing/labeling artifact, or a genuine but coincidental ordering.
- Whether the earlier 38.40/52.60 min summaries reflect different file versions, different analysis populations, or arithmetic errors.
- Whether enrollment, concealment, and consent were implemented as described.
- Whether adverse events outside CMC (including readmissions to other hospitals) were captured.
- Whether the "Hemoclip" export label corresponds to Hem-o-Lok, and whether the titanium clip is LT400-equivalent (model, lot, applier, count).
- Exact recruitment dates and follow-up closure dates (extract contains none).

### 4.4 Tables and figures

- Table 1 (baseline): age and sex only; balanced by design/record, but no severity or case-mix variables (e.g., BMI, ASA, inflammation grade, operative complexity).
- Table 2 (outcomes): adequately structured but footnoted with verification caveats; median stay identical (2 days) while means differ — both should be reported (done), but discharge criteria unavailable.
- Figure 1: scatter of operative time; marker area proportional to frequency; label caveat included.
- Figure 2: explicit caveat that cross-study comparison with Uçar & Bulut [10] precludes pooling or causal comparison — appropriately cautious, but the figure adds no decision-relevant evidence and risks implying comparability.
- Figure 3: nominal risk differences and Newcombe intervals; appropriately labeled as assuming independent groups.

### 4.5 Recommendations

1. Report primary analysis as ITT by assigned group; add per-protocol and as-treated sensitivity analyses conditional on source verification.
2. Present unadjusted and (if prespecified) baseline-adjusted analyses; report effect estimates with CIs, not merely nominal intervals.
3. Report the r = 0.993 pattern transparently as a data-quality anomaly pending source-record explanation; do not present nominal CIs as confirmatory until resolved.
4. Reconcile or formally retract the conflicting 38.40/52.60 min summaries; document which file version was analyzed.
5. Define complication ascertainment, severity grading (e.g., Clavien–Dindo), and observation windows; report harms per CONSORT Harms 2022 [11].
6. Withdraw any claim of safety equivalence; replace with "safety could not be established from sparse, unverified events."
7. If the data cannot be verified, reframe the manuscript as a protocol-development/audit report rather than a confirmatory RCT report.

## 5. STRENGTHENING THE DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS

### 5.1 Depth and accuracy of interpretation

The Discussion appropriately avoids interpreting the contrast as a confirmed treatment effect ("the provenance and allocation of the underlying observations must be established before the estimate can be interpreted clinically," p.6) and correctly notes that total operative time is not stump-closure time. These are strengths.

Weaknesses:
- The abstract's Conclusion nonetheless reads as a verdict ("The supplied extract shows shorter recorded total operative times in the titanium group") without leading with the verification caveat.
- Mechanistic speculation is limited but present ("would be relevant to operating-room workflow if confirmed"); cost savings are mentioned as unmeasured yet implicitly motivating.
- The comparison with prior work (Lee et al. [15]; Shaikh et al. [16]; Uçar & Bulut [10]) is descriptive; it does not synthesize effect sizes or explain the direction/magnitude discrepancy.

### 5.2 Connection to previous research

- Uçar & Bulut (2024) near-null result (53.1 vs 52.4 min) is acknowledged as "a much smaller contrast than in the supplied extract" but not reconciled — a major omission given it is the only published direct titanium-vs-Hem-o-Lok head-to-head.
- The DS clip trial [9] and TICAP study [13] are discussed regarding applicability limits (base size, cecal inflammation), which supports the exclusion criteria but does not generalize to the study population as enrolled.
- Rasmussen et al. [29] concerns appendectomy generally, not comparative late clip safety — correctly scoped.

### 5.3 Limitations

Limitations appear late (Discussion pp.6–8) and are appropriately extensive (single surgeon, single center, ten-year period, unverified follow-up, no noninferiority margin, no pediatric/complicated-appendicitis inference). However, the **most important limitation — unverifiable randomization conduct and anomalous data pattern — is buried in the Data reconciliation subsection rather than headlined in Limitations**, and the manuscript never states that the primary estimate may be invalid if the data pattern reflects non-independence or manipulation.

### 5.4 Implications

Theoretical implications: none established (mechanism not measured). Practical implications: conditional workflow relevance only. Cost implications explicitly unmeasured — should not be implied.

### 5.5 Conclusions

The submitted Conclusion (p.9) is appropriately hedged: "Recorded total operative time was shorter in the titanium group in the supplied extract… Verification of allocation, follow-up completeness and the discrepancy with earlier analytical outputs remains necessary before interpreting the contrast as a randomized treatment effect. These records do not establish equivalent safety." This is the strongest section of the manuscript and should be preserved and tightened in revision.

### 5.6 Recommendations

1. Lead the Discussion with the verification status: state plainly that results are a provisional reanalysis of an unverified extract.
2. Reconcile the Uçar & Bulut [10] discrepancy explicitly; if irreconcilable, downgrade claims to hypothesis-generating.
3. Promote the data-integrity anomaly (r = 0.993; constant arm offset) to a headline limitation.
4. Remove any implication of cost savings or mechanistic advantage (not measured).
5. Restructure Limitations into: design limitations (single center/surgeon), measurement limitations (total time vs closure time), data limitations (unverified allocation, missing follow-up windows, sparse events), and analysis limitations (no ITT verification, no prespecified SAP).
6. State clearly that no safety or effectiveness claim is supported pending verification.
7. Align conclusions with CONSORT 2025 [30] and CONSORT Harms 2022 [11].

## 6. ACADEMIC WRITING AND ORGANIZATION

### 6.1 Clarity, coherence, and logical flow

The manuscript is generally readable, with conventional sectioning (Abstract, Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusion, Declarations, References). Logical flow is acceptable, but the document reads partly like an internal audit memo ("should be verified," "remains to be confirmed," "not established") rather than a research article, because verification status is interwoven with reporting rather than segregated.

### 6.2 Precision of terminology

- "Study codes" and "EnvelopeNo" are export artifacts, not research terms; their meaning requires explanation.
- "Hemoclip" is the source-file label, not a device name; the brand (Hem-o-Lok) is unverified. The manuscript switches between "Hemoclip," "Hem-o-Lok," and "polymeric locking clip" without a fixed convention.
- "LT400-equivalent" is undefined; equivalence to what standard is unstated.
- "10-Year" in the title is misleading: the export identifies calendar years 2017–2026, but no exact dates; the "ten-year designation refers to the described study period, not ten years of observation per participant" (Discussion, p.8). The title should not claim ten years of follow-up.

### 6.3 Redundancy

- The verification caveat is repeated in Abstract, Methods, Results, Discussion, and Conclusion. This is appropriate for a draft but should be consolidated in revision (e.g., a single "Data verification status" note plus a limitations section).
- The Uçar & Bulut and clip–endoloop literature is summarized twice (Introduction pp.2–3 and Discussion pp.6–7).

### 6.4 Grammar, tone, and style

Tone is formal and appropriately cautious. No egregious grammatical errors were detected in the extracted text. Minor issues: mixed UK/US spelling is not evident; passive constructions dominate (acceptable in surgical writing); "nominal 95% CI" is used correctly but should be defined once.

### 6.5 Recommendations

1. Adopt a fixed nomenclature: specify device names (manufacturer, model, catalog number) once, then use consistently; replace "Hemoclip" with the verified device name or retain "recorded group label" with a footnote.
2. Add a structured abstract (Background/Methods/Results/Conclusion) per CONSORT abstract extensions.
3. Add a CONSORT 2025-compliant flow diagram and a trial registry identifier (or explicit statement of non-registration with justification).
4. Copyedit for conciseness; merge duplicated verification caveats into a "Data provenance and verification" subsection.
5. Define all abbreviations at first use (SD, IQR, CI, pp, ITT, CONSORT).

## 7. SIMULATED JOURNAL PEER-REVIEW REPORT AND EDITORIAL RECOMMENDATION

### 7.1 Overall assessment

The manuscript addresses a legitimate clinical question in an appropriately cautious tone and cites current standards (CONSORT 2025, CONSORT Harms 2022). However, as submitted it is a **working draft whose central claims cannot be verified**. The randomization, blinding, intervention identity, outcomes ascertainment, analysis population, trial registration, and protocol/SAP are all unverified or absent; the data contain an unexplained anomalous cross-arm pattern; earlier analytical outputs conflict with the analyzed export; and the primary outcome cannot isolate the effect of the device. Under CONSORT 2025 and ICMJE standards, a randomized trial report must present verifiable registration, protocol, flow, and analysis — none of which can be confirmed here.

### 7.2 Major concerns (must be addressed before any acceptance consideration)
