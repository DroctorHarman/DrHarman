## ABSTRACT (STRUCTURED, CONSORT-ABSTRACT FORMAT)

**Background:** Mechanical clips are an alternative to ligatures and staplers for appendiceal stump closure during laparoscopic appendectomy, but evidence directly comparing titanium ligation clips with non-absorbable polymeric locking clips is limited and conflicting. We report a prespecified reanalysis of a single-center, single-surgeon randomized trial comparing total operative time and short-term outcomes with the two devices.

**Methods:** Prospective, two-arm, 1:1 parallel randomized trial at CMC Private Hospital, Erbil, Iraq, conducted by one surgeon. Adults and children (≥ 6 years) with uncomplicated acute appendicitis and a clearly identifiable appendiceal base ≤ 10 mm were eligible; severe base inflammation or necrosis, interval surgery, suspected neoplasm, pregnancy, and indications for primary open surgery were excluded. Randomization was described as computer-generated blocks of 10 using opaque, sequentially numbered envelopes opened after intraoperative eligibility assessment [allocation log pending verification, E3]. The intervention was a large titanium ligation clip (described as LT400-equivalent) versus a non-absorbable polymeric locking clip (Hem-o-Lok), both within a standardized three-port approach [device details pending verification, E4]. The primary endpoint was total operative time (skin incision to final skin closure). Secondary endpoints were complications, hospital stay, conversion to open surgery, and 30-day readmission. Analysis was by recorded treatment group; intention-to-treat status is not established [E2]. Intervals are nominal and unadjusted and assume independent observations [independence pending verification, E8].

**Results:** The analysis export comprised 200 recorded study codes, 100 per recorded group. Recorded mean operative time was 43.28 minutes (SD 8.05) with titanium versus 56.72 minutes (SD 9.24) with the polymeric clip (recorded-group difference −13.44 minutes; nominal 95% CI −15.86 to −11.02). Recorded median operative time was 42 minutes [IQR 36.75–50] versus 55 minutes [IQR 49–64]. Recorded mean hospital stay was 1.85 days (SD 0.78) versus 2.22 days (SD 0.79) (difference −0.37 days; nominal 95% CI −0.59 to −0.15); medians were 2 days [IQR 1–2] versus 2 days [IQR 2–3]. Recorded complications occurred in 6 (6%) versus 7 (7) patients (risk difference −1.0 percentage point; nominal 95% CI −8.5 to 6.4). Recorded complications comprised superficial wound infection (2 vs 2), prolonged ileus (2 vs 2), urinary retention (1 vs 1), seroma (1 vs 1), and atelectasis (0 vs 1). No open conversions were recorded. One 30-day readmission was recorded in the comparator group and none in the titanium group.

**Interpretation:** Recorded total operative time was shorter in the titanium group in the supplied extract, but the estimate cannot yet be interpreted as a randomized treatment effect: allocation concealment, blinding, device identity, follow-up completeness, and the discrepancy with earlier analytical outputs (means of 38.40 and 52.60 minutes in an earlier summary) remain to be verified against source records [E1–E9]. The sparse complication data do not establish safety equivalence. These findings are hypothesis-generating pending verification.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Acute appendicitis is among the most common surgical emergencies, and laparoscopic appendectomy remains the standard operative approach for most patients [1, 2]. Antibiotic-first management is an option for selected patients with uncomplicated disease [1], but when appendectomy is indicated, the technical steps with the greatest potential for variation include mesoappendix division and appendiceal stump closure [14]. Secure stump closure is essential: inadequate closure risks stump leak, intra-abdominal abscess, and readmission, whereas excessive operative time increases theatre occupancy and cost. Ligatures, endostaplers, and mechanical clips differ in deployment, anatomy of application, cost, and learning curve [4, 27].

Clip application for laparoscopic appendectomy was described in early clinical work [5], and a Cochrane review judged the evidence insufficient to recommend any specific mechanical closure device over another [6]. Evidence syntheses describe substantial variation in devices, patient selection, and outcome definitions [7, 8]. Direct device comparisons exist: a four-arm randomized trial compared a titanium DS clip with Hem-o-Lok, an endoloop, and a stapler [9]; a retrospective head-to-head study of 179 patients compared titanium clips with Hem-o-Lok and reported nearly identical mean operative times (53.1 vs 52.4 minutes) [10]; randomized and nonrandomized studies have assessed polymeric clips against endoloops [15–17, 19, 21–23], ligatures [24, 25], and endostaplers [27]; and meta-analyses synthesize the clip-versus-endoloop literature [16, 18, 26]. None of these established the effect of a titanium ligation clip versus a non-absorbable polymeric locking clip on total operative time in a setting like CMC Private Hospital, and the only directly comparable head-to-head [10] reports a near-null contrast that is in sharp tension with much larger differences reported anecdotally in local practice.

The clinical question addressed here is whether two locally used clip strategies — a large titanium ligation clip (described as LT400-equivalent) versus a non-absorbable polymeric locking clip (Hem-o-Lok) — differ in total operative duration and short-term harms in laparoscopic appendectomy. Total operative time was selected as the primary endpoint because it is objectively recorded and directly relevant to theatre workflow; however, total operative time is not stump-closure time, and any observed difference cannot be attributed to the clip without adjustment for dissection, instrumentation, and case complexity. This manuscript reports a provisional reanalysis of a supplied 200-row extract; trial conduct and device identity remain to be verified against source records before any causal interpretation.

**Research objectives:** (1) to compare recorded total operative time between titanium and polymeric locking clips for appendiceal stump closure; (2) to compare recorded short-term outcomes (complications, hospital stay, conversion to open surgery, 30-day readmission); and (3) to document the verification status of allocation, intervention fidelity, and follow-up.

## 2. METHODS

### 2.1 Study design and setting

This was a prospective, two-arm, 1:1 parallel-group randomized trial conducted by a single surgeon at CMC Private Hospital, Erbil, Iraq. The described recruitment period spans calendar years 2017–2026 [exact dates pending verification, E5]. The single-surgeon design reduces between-operator variation but limits generalizability; over a long recruitment period, operative experience, protocols, and equipment may change, constituting a threat to internal validity that is acknowledged in the limitations. The "ten-year" designation in prior drafts refers to the described study period, not to ten years of observation per participant; no participant was followed for ten years.

### 2.2 Participants

**Eligibility (inclusion):** age ≥ 6 years; uncomplicated acute appendicitis; clearly identifiable appendiceal base no more than 10 mm in diameter.

**Exclusions:** severe base inflammation or necrosis; interval surgery; suspected neoplasm; pregnancy; indications for primary open surgery.

The numbers screened, excluded before assignment, and verified as randomized in the original enrollment records were not available [CONSORT flow pending reconstruction, E6]. Pediatric enrolment (age ≥ 6 years) means that any efficacy or safety conclusion is limited to children and adults who met eligibility; the study is not powered for subgroup analyses.

### 2.3 Randomization and allocation concealment

Randomization was described as computer-generated blocks of 10 using opaque, sequentially numbered envelopes opened after intraoperative eligibility assessment — a design appropriate to concealment in principle. Implementation, however, cannot be verified: the author confirmed that all 200 EnvelopeNo values were reassigned by treatment arm for the analysis export (001–100 Titanium; 101–200 Hemoclip), and no subset retains a documented original opening-order number [E3]. The original allocation sequence and the implementation of concealment must be confirmed from the allocation log before the randomized design can be considered established.

The surgeon could not be masked to the assigned device (the device is visible at application). Masking of participants, outcome assessors, and data analysts is unverified [E1]. Lack of verified blinding is a detection-bias risk for ascertainment-dependent complications (wound infection, ileus, readmission).

### 2.4 Interventions

- **Titanium arm:** large titanium ligation clip, described as LT400-equivalent [device identity pending verification, E4].
- **Polymeric arm:** non-absorbable polymeric locking clip, described as Hem-o-Lok [the export label is "Hemoclip"; brand identity pending verification, E4].

Both groups reportedly underwent a standardized three-port laparoscopic appendectomy. Model, size, applier, and the number of clips actually used per case should be verified from operative records and device documentation [E4]. The row-level export distinguishes only "Titanium" and "Hemoclip"; standardized nomenclature must be adopted in the final manuscript.

### 2.5 Outcomes

**Primary endpoint:** total operative time, defined as skin incision to final skin closure (minutes). This is an objective, theatre-recorded measure, but it is *not* stump-closure time; dissection, instrumentation, and case complexity are not separated.

**Secondary endpoints:**
- Hospital stay (days), from operation to discharge;
- Complications, by type and (pending verification) severity grade;
- Conversion to open surgery;
- 30-day readmission.

**Ascertainment and windows (pending confirmation, E9):** the complication ascertainment method, observation window, severity grading system, and whether care outside CMC Private Hospital was captured were not established by the export. Follow-up dates in the separately supplied table are relative to discharge; whether readmission was counted from surgery or discharge remains unconfirmed. Zero counts in the export indicate "no positive flags in the export," not verified absence of events, consistent with CONSORT Harms 2022 guidance that zero counts be reported only where systematic assessment is documented [11].

### 2.6 Sample size

No prespecified sample-size calculation was available [E2]. With 200 recorded codes (100 per arm) and 13 total complication events, the study is substantially underpowered for safety outcomes; no noninferiority or equivalence margin was prespecified [28]. Consequently, the study can generate hypotheses about operative time but cannot establish safety equivalence or non-inferiority of either device.

### 2.7 Data collection and management

The analysis was performed on a supplied 200-row extract as delivered, by recorded group. No missing values were found in the fields needed for the reported calculations. The extract contains total operative minutes but no source timestamps; calendar dates are absent [E5]. The separately supplied follow-up table records clinic follow-up as completed for all 200 codes, with dates relative to discharge. Data management, coding, and deviation handling cannot be audited without the protocol and analysis log [E2].

### 2.8 Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are summarized as means and standard deviations, with medians and interquartile ranges for operative time and stay; categorical variables as counts and percentages. Contrasts are expressed as titanium minus polymeric clip. Mean differences were estimated with Welch intervals; risk differences with Newcombe score intervals. All intervals are nominal, unadjusted, and assume independent observations [independence pending verification, E8].

**Estimand:** the primary estimand is the difference in recorded mean total operative time by assigned treatment group. Intention-to-treat analysis is not established [E2]; per-protocol and as-treated sensitivity analyses cannot be presented until the allocation log is verified [E3]. Operative time is a post-allocation variable; no post-randomization covariates are adjusted for in the primary analysis. No adjusted complication model was carried forward given 13 events. No formal noninferiority or equivalence testing was performed [28].

All analyses are provisional and descriptive; statistical significance is not claimed.

### 2.9 Ethical considerations

The Research Ethics Committee of CMC Private Hospital approved the protocol on 3 January 2016 (approval no. 202), requiring written informed consent from participants or their legal guardians before enrollment and randomization. Consent and pediatric assent procedures as implemented require confirmation [E1]. Trial registration status is unconfirmed [E1]. This research received no funding. ## 3. RESULTS

### 3.1 Participant flow and analysis population

A CONSORT 2025 flow diagram cannot be constructed from the supplied documents because counts screened, excluded before assignment, and verified as randomized were not available [E6]. The descriptive analysis included 200 recorded study codes, 100 per recorded group. The supplied allocation export lists 100 codes assigned to each arm and 100 recorded as receiving the recorded assigned clip in each arm, with no recorded crossovers. The separate follow-up table records clinic follow-up as completed for 100 per arm, with none marked lost. The analysis uses the recorded group labels; it is **not** presented as a verified intention-to-treat analysis [E2, E3].

### 3.2 Baseline characteristics

Table 1 reports the only baseline fields available in the export: age and sex. Groups were balanced on these fields (mean age 39.55 vs 41.24 years; 50% male in each arm). Case-mix variables potentially relevant to operative time — inflammation grade, ASA score, body mass index, appendiceal base diameter, operative complexity — were not available in the export and should be added from source records [E6].

### 3.3 Primary outcome: operative time

Recorded mean operative time was 43.28 minutes (SD 8.05) in the titanium group versus 56.72 minutes (SD 9.24) in the polymeric-clip group (recorded-group difference −13.44 minutes; nominal Welch 95% CI −15.86 to −11.02). Recorded medians were 42 minutes [IQR 36.75–50] versus 55 minutes [IQR 49–64] (Table 2; Figure 1). The Welch interval is arithmetically consistent with the reported means and standard deviations under the independence assumption (standard error ≈ 1.23 minutes), but independence of the two arms cannot presently be verified [E8].

**Cautionary findings:** (i) earlier analytical summaries report mean operative times of 38.40 and 52.60 minutes, which are not reproduced by the supplied row-level records; the cause of the discrepancy is unknown and the summaries remain unreconciled [E7]; (ii) after alignment of group-specific identifiers by numeric suffix, cross-arm operative times were highly correlated (r = 0.993), with every comparator value 10–18 minutes higher than the matched titanium value [E8]. Shared suffixes do not establish clinical pairing, and this pattern — whether reflecting a labeling artifact, dependent records, or another explanation — must be resolved from source records before any inferential claim.

### 3.4 Secondary outcomes

Recorded mean hospital stay was 1.85 days (SD 0.78) versus 2.22 days (SD 0.79) (difference −0.37 days; nominal 95% CI −0.59 to −0.15); recorded medians were 2 days [IQR 1–2] versus 2 days [IQR 2–3]. The divergence between mean and median stay warrants reporting of both; discharge criteria were not available.

Recorded complications: 6 (6%) versus 7 (7) (risk difference −1.0 percentage point; nominal Newcombe 95% CI −8.5 to 6.4). Recorded complication subtypes: superficial wound infection (2 vs 2), prolonged ileus (2 vs 2), urinary retention (1 vs 1), seroma (1 vs 1), atelectasis (0 vs 1). Severity grades and stump-specific ascertainment could not be verified [E9].

Recorded conversions to open surgery: 0 versus 0. Recorded 30-day readmission: 0 (titanium) versus 1 (comparator) (risk difference −1.0 percentage point; nominal 95% CI −5.4 to 2.8). One comparator readmission was confirmed against the follow-up record after reconciliation [this correction did not change the operative-time comparison]; zeroes indicate no positive flags in the export, not verified absence of events.

### 3.5 Allocation-record review

The supplied allocation export lists 100 study codes per assigned arm, with assigned and received clips matching on every row and no recorded crossovers. The author confirmed that all 200 EnvelopeNo values were reassigned by treatment arm for the export (001–100 Titanium; 101–200 Hemoclip); no subset retains a documented original opening-order number. The export documents recorded assignments and recorded clips received but cannot independently verify the original allocation sequence or concealment implementation [E3].

### 3.6 Data reconciliation

The author designated the analysis export as final and confirmed one corrected 30-day readmission against the follow-up record; the correction did not change the operative-time comparison. The provenance of conflicting earlier outputs remains unresolved [E7]. The separately supplied 38.40- and 52.60-minute means appear in an earlier summary and are not reproduced by the supplied row-level records [43.28 vs 56.72 minutes]. A difference between reported means does not by itself establish different file versions; source records are required [E7].

## 4. DISCUSSION

### 4.1 Summary of findings

In this provisional reanalysis of a single-center, single-surgeon randomized-trial extract, recorded total operative time was approximately 13 minutes shorter with the titanium clip than with the non-absorbable polymeric locking clip (recorded mean 43.28 vs 56.72 minutes; nominal 95% CI −15.86 to −11.02). Recorded hospital stay was shorter by 0.37 days on average while medians were identical (2 days). Recorded complications were infrequent and balanced (6 vs 7 events), with no open conversions and one recorded 30-day readmission in the comparator group only.

### 4.2 Interpretation and comparison with previous research

The magnitude of the operative-time difference would be relevant to theatre workflow **if confirmed**, but three conditions limit interpretation:

1. **Endpoint validity.** Total operative time is not stump-closure time. The estimate conflates clip loading/deployment with dissection, instrumentation, and case complexity; it cannot isolate the effect of the device. A prior randomized trial of a distinct titanium DS-clip design found applicability limited by severe cecal inflammation [9, 12, 13], and a randomized clip–endoloop trial found shorter instrument-application-to-cutting intervals with clips but no significant difference in total surgery time [15] — evidence that closure-device effects on total operative time are modest and inconsistent.

2. **Conflict with the only directly comparable head-to-head.** Uçar and Bulut [10] reported mean operative times of 53.1 minutes (titanium) versus 52.4 minutes (Hem-o-Lok) in 179 patients — a near-null contrast in the opposite direction to the −13.44-minute difference recorded here. This discrepancy is more consequential than any choice of statistical test: the provenance and allocation of the underlying observations must be established before the estimate can be interpreted clinically. Meta-analyses of clips versus ligatures or endoloops [16, 18, 26] and single-device feasibility series [21–23, 25] cannot resolve the titanium-versus-Hem-o-Lok question.

3. **Design and conduct limitations.** Single surgeon, single center, unverified allocation concealment, unverified blinding of assessors, unverified device identity, an unreconciled discrepancy with earlier summaries, and an anomalous cross-arm correlation (r = 0.993) [E3, E4, E7, E8].

Mechanistic explanations (clip deployment speed, locking security) and cost savings were not measured; implications for cost or workflow efficiency are therefore speculative.

### 4.3 Limitations

- **Data verification.** The manuscript reports a provisional reanalysis of an unverified extract; allocation concealment, blinding, intervention fidelity, and follow-up completeness require source-record confirmation [E1–E5].
- **Allocation integrity.** Envelope numbers were reassigned by arm in the export; the original opening order is not reconstructable [E3]. The r = 0.993 cross-arm correlation with a constant arm offset remains unexplained [E8].
- **Analytical.** No intention-to-treat analysis; no prespecified analysis plan; nominal unadjusted intervals assuming independence; no adjustment for case mix [E2, E8].
- **Outcome measurement.** Primary endpoint is total operative time, not closure time; complication ascertainment, severity grading, and observation windows unverified; readmission counting origin (surgery vs discharge) unconfirmed; out-of-hospital events potentially uncaptured [E9].
- **Statistical power.** 13 complication events; no noninferiority/equivalence margin; safety equivalence not established [28].
- **Generalizability.** Single center, single surgeon, eligibility restricted to uncomplicated appendicitis with base ≤ 10 mm; no inference to complicated appendicitis, late device-related events, or pediatric subgroups beyond those enrolled; long-term outcomes after appendectomy (ileus, incisional hernia) concern appendectomy generally, not comparative clip safety [29].
- **Temporal.** The described period spans 2017–2026; secular changes in experience, protocols, and equipment may affect within-study comparability [E5].

### 4.4 Implications

**Practice:** No change in practice is supported. The recorded difference is hypothesis-generating only.  
**Research:** A verified, adequately powered, multicenter randomized trial with a prespecified estimand (stump-closure time or total operative time), registered protocol, auditable allocation, blinded outcome assessment where feasible, and a prespecified noninferiority/equivalence safety margin is required before either device can be preferred. Reporting should follow CONSORT 2025 [30] and CONSORT Harms 2022 [11].

### 4.5 Conclusion

Recorded total operative time was shorter in the titanium group in the supplied extract, and the author confirmed the final analysis export and the correction of one readmission against the follow-up record. Verification of allocation, follow-up completeness, and the discrepancy with earlier analytical outputs remains necessary before interpreting the contrast as a randomized treatment effect. These records do not establish equivalent safety, and no effectiveness or safety claim is supported pending source-data verification.
